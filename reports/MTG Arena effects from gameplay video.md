# Arena's feel comes from colour grammar, not spectacle

Frame-by-frame reads of 14 MTG Arena gameplay videos (13 uploaded September-October 2026, one from May 2025; Standard and Draft) show that Arena's effects are **short, colour-coded and never shake the screen**. Every machine-driven step takes 0.3-1.5 s: release-to-stack takes about 0.5 s, mana payment about 0.5 s, a death about 1 s, a life change under 1 s. What stretches a game is player decision time. The visual language is a strict grammar. **Cyan** means "can act, valid target, or targeting". **Orange-gold** means "chosen, committed, or paying". **Red-orange** means attacking or damage, **gold lines** mean blocks, **green** means life gain, **blue** means a boosted P/T or a counter badge, **purple-black smoke** means death and **white-grey smoke** means countered. Damage numbers stay the same size from 1 to 11 damage. What scales is time, because each attacker gets its own flight and impact. Almost all of this is reachable on our TTS Commander table without a single AssetBundle, using `highlightOn`, pre-declared XML UI, Object UI numbers on the life trackers, and drawn lines. Pooled particle rigs only matter for four moments: death, counterspell, impact and elimination. The table already matches Arena in five places: the playable glow, the per-seat YOUR TURN banner, the right-edge stack pop-up, the persistent red/blue attacker and blocker glows, and the orange-primary/blue-secondary corner buttons. The biggest gaps are the lines Arena draws for targets, attacks and blocks, because on a four-player table "who is this aimed at" matters far more than in Arena's 1v1. The spec's planned camera shake should be dropped: Arena does not shake.

## Fourteen videos, sampled at random, show one consistent rulebook

The evidence comes from three passes over YouTube videos, each 34-89 minutes long, with no two videos the same. Each pass seeked the paused video to random start points, then stepped through 0.07-0.5 s at a time around events, so timings are accurate to roughly ±0.1-0.25 s. Sources include PowrDragn, CovertGoBlue, Sonio and AbojiiMTG for spells ([V1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE), [V4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MWesh9bEQNE)), MTGJace, PowrDragn, Jay Villain and Tim Johnson for combat ([MTGJace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4), [Gruul](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k)), and Paul Cheon, NumotTheNummy and LSV for turn flow and endings ([LSV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI), [Numot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0)). Three limits apply throughout. **There is no sound data**: captions held only the streamers' speech. **There is no Brawl footage**, so nothing about command-zone casts or commander damage. Several random windows fell on deck-building or menus.

Three rules hold across every video. First, **no camera or screen shake**. Board, avatars and neighbouring cards kept the same pixel positions through every impact, from 0.07 s steps on a creature kill to a lethal 4-creature swing ([MTGJace 389.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=389s); [Gruul 292 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k&t=292s)). Second, **the two-step colour grammar is used everywhere**: cyan for "available", switching to orange-gold within about 0.1 s of a click. The same colours mark hand cards, attackers, targets and mana sources ([MTGJace 364.8-364.9 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=364s); [V1 33:17.9-33:18.3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=1997s)). Third, **big effects are brief and persistent cues are small**. On boards with 10-14 permanents per side, the tapped look, counter badges, blue P/T and attack chevrons stayed small and in fixed spots. Big effects either lasted 0.25-1.5 s or opened a modal that dimmed the board ([Numot 5010 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0&t=5010s); [PowrDragn 1044 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=1044s)).

| Meaning | Arena colour | Our table today (`tools/engine-renderer.lua`) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Can act: playable card, eligible attacker, valid target | Cyan outline | Green frame on playable hand cards; **white** for eligible attackers and blockers; **orange** for valid targets | Same idea, three different colours. Unify on one "available" colour |
| Chosen, committed or paying | Orange-gold outline | Gold on lands to tap; **red** on a picked target or attacker; blue on a picked blocker | Mana matches. Picked targets should turn gold, not red |
| Attacking | Orange-red chevron, red tint, red-pink line to the defender | Persistent red glow | Matches; the line is missing |
| Blocking | Thin gold line, blocker to attacker | Persistent blue glow | Glow is fine; the line is missing |
| Targeting link | Cyan arc, stack card to target | None | Missing; cyan is free on our table |
| Damage / life loss | Red number, red tint on the life plate | None | Missing |
| Life gain | Green rising number, green column | None | Missing |
| Death | White flash, then purple-black smoke and shatter | None | Missing |
| Countered | Grey card, then a white-grey smoke burst | None | Missing |
| Boosted P/T, counters | Blue P/T text, dark-blue badge | Not checked | Check |
| Your turn / another player's turn | Gold-orange / blue-white banner | Gold for both | Small fix |

Our orange "valid target" is the inverse of Arena: orange is Arena's "already chosen" colour. Fixing that is a two-line change to the `GLOW` table. I recommend keeping **green** rather than switching to cyan as the "available" colour, because blue already marks blockers and purple marks stack items. Cyan and blue next to each other would blur on a busy four-player board. The grammar matters more than the hue: one colour for "you could pick this", one for "this is picked", and persistent state colours that never mean either.

## A spell's life takes about four machine seconds

A cast has six visible stages. In the clearest sample, a blue-black modal charm, the card rose from the hand with a **cyan outline** and two lands lit **orange** (the auto-tap preview). Held over the board, the card's outline turned **orange-gold**, and it settled in the stack slot at the right edge **≤0.9 s** after leaving the hand ([V1 33:17.9-33:18.8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=1997s)). A dimmed "Choose One" overlay followed, then the prompt "Target a creature." with the only legal target outlined in cyan ([V1 33:19.3-33:20.3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=1999s)). Mana payment came next. Each tapped land spun a vortex orb in its mana colour (**cyan for the Island, violet-magenta for the Swamp**) and sent a sparkling ribbon to the stack card. At the same moment a **bright cyan arc** rose from the target and curved over to the stack ([V1 33:20.8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=2000s)). The charm resolved about 3.5 s after it was cast. The target died under a white sphere with blue wisps, then dark smoke, and was gone 1.3 s after the effect began ([V1 33:21.45-33:22.7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=2001s)).

Mana colour carries through consistently. A white creature's three lands flared **gold-white**, then thick **gold beams** crossed the whole board into the stack card. The whole payment took **0.5-0.6 s** ([V2 12:44.05-12:44.6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=764s)). An opponent's Forests flashed **lime-green** with lime beams ([V2 37:21.3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=2241s)), and a single Island sent one long horizontal cyan beam ([V4 6:33.6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MWesh9bEQNE&t=393s)). A gold-frame spell got one beam per land, each in its own colour. **Red and colourless payments were never caught on camera.** No sample showed effects scaling with mana value: spells costing 1, 2 and 3 used the same stack size.

The **stack** is a single slot at the right edge, centred near x 0.90, y 0.45 of the frame. The top item is shown at about **2× battlefield-card width** (about 18% of frame width), as a full, readable card. Older items peek out behind it, offset up-left and rotated a few degrees ([V1 1994 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=1994s)). Triggered and loyalty abilities use the same frame with the type line "Ability" and the source's art; loyalty abilities add a cost badge ([V2 3514 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=3514s)). Your items and the opponent's look the same. Ownership shows only in the bottom-right button ("Resolve" / "Resolve All" versus "Opponent's Turn"). An item stays on the stack until both players pass. One creature sat there unchanged for **28 s** while the opponent thought ([V2 774 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=774s)). Some stack items carried auras (a purple flame swirl, a gold ring, an electric-blue flame) and others had none. With one sample of each, no rule linking aura to owner or colour was found.

A **resolving creature** leaves the slot flying and shrinking. A small grey puff stays behind, and a dark drop-shadow already marks its landing spot. The card lands in about 0.3 s and then shows a **white-blue flash** over the whole card ([V2 13:11.75-13:12.5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=791s)). A **countered spell** looks different. In the one clear example, the counter flew in front of a red sorcery and a short cyan arc curved back to the sorcery behind it. On resolution the counter turned desaturated with a white rim. Then a **large white-grey smoke cloud with dark debris flecks** filled the stack slot, the red card vanished inside it, and the counter appeared on its owner's graveyard. The whole sequence took **2.5-3.4 s** from desaturation to clear ([V4 6:41.0-6:43.5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MWesh9bEQNE&t=401s)). That burst is far bigger than the small puff a normal resolution leaves.

**Targeting lines are curved, glowing cyan ribbons**, thicker in the middle and sometimes shading to white at the far end. They join the stack item to its target and stay up while the item waits. When the target is a player, both life bubbles turn cyan while the player chooses ([V1 2559.2 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=2559s); [V2 761.2 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCs2sTvs5jc&t=761s)). No arrowheads were seen in still frames.

## Combat is told with lines and flights, and death with purple smoke

Declaring attackers shows the prompt "Choose attackers." with "No Attacks" / "All Attack" pills. Eligible creatures glow cyan. A clicked creature turns **orange within about 0.1 s** and grows an orange-red **chevron "roof"** on the edge facing the opponent. On confirm, attackers slide forward 0.2-0.3 card-heights and take a red-orange, darkened tint ([MTGJace 364.5-366.3 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=364s)). A **thin red-pink line with a white core** runs from each attacker to the defending player ([V1 2559.9 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKNToSagdHE&t=2559s)) or curves to an attacked planeswalker. One such line stayed up **about 9 s** while players held priority ([Jay Villain 2398-2407 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QtQ1j1Di9bw&t=2398s)). **Blocks are thin golden lines**, from a bright point on each blocker to the top edge of its attacker. Two blockers on a menace attacker gave two converging lines. With several blockers, a dimmed "Assign Damage" modal opens with a number box on each blocker ([MTGJace 373.9-379.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=373s)).

**Combat damage to a player is physical.** Each attacker lifts, tilts back and flies into the defender's portrait, trailing a white-blue streak. On contact there is an orange-white starburst about two avatar-widths across and a red damage number about a third of the portrait's height. The life total **snaps** to its new value (18 → 10, no count-down) and the life plate tints red for about 0.5 s; then the card flies back ([Gruul 293.3-294.0 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k&t=293s)). Flights take about 0.3 s each way. The next attacker launches 0.4-0.7 s after the previous impact in one video and 1.2-1.5 s in another. The red number fades in about 0.45 s ([MTGJace 1622.0-1624.55 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=1622s)). Totals go negative ("-12"). A **ping from an ability** runs a cyan arc, then a red-orange comet (0.15-0.3 s), a red-white starburst, a red "1" under a golden light pillar, and finally the life switch. From launch to the tint clearing takes about **1.7 s** ([MTGJace 366.5-368.65 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=366s)).

**Damage to creatures** starts when the attacker lunges into its blockers. Gold sparks fly at the contact point, a white flash bursts around each damaged card, and a small red number (about the size of the P/T box) appears near the P/T ([MTGJace 379.6-380.9 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=379s)). A burn spell's flash spread over 2-3 card-widths, but neighbouring cards did not move. **Damage to a planeswalker** puts a red "4" beside the loyalty shield. The shield itself updated about 1.8 s later, from 6 to 2, with a blue outline glow ([Jay Villain 2407.4-2409.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QtQ1j1Di9bw&t=2407s)).

**Death is one generic effect**, the same for combat and spell kills: a white flash, then a purple orb in billowing **dark purple-black smoke about 1.5× the card's size**. The card face darkens, breaks into square tiles and scatters, and the slot is empty about **0.9-1.0 s** after impact. The surviving cards then slide together over 0.3-0.5 s ([MTGJace 389.69-391.0 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=389s)). Two simultaneous deaths overlapped, about 0.3 s apart ([MTGJace 380.5-381.9 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=380s)). The card was never seen flying to the graveyard; it simply disappears.

**Life changes are cheap and readable.** Life gain shows a green "+1" rising from the right of the life plate by about a portrait-height in 0.45 s, through a green light column with sparkles. The whole effect lasts about **0.75 s** and then the total snaps 25 → 26 ([Jay Villain 2430.0-2430.75 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QtQ1j1Di9bw&t=2430s)). Paying life for a shock land gave only a red-pink flash on the portrait and an orange glow at its base. The total switched and no number appeared ([Gruul 1642.8-1643.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k&t=1641s)).

## Turn, priority and board-state cues stay small and fixed

A turn start unfolds in this order. The lands lose their grey tapped look within about 0.5 s. The portrait frame lights orange, with phase pips on both sides. "**YOUR TURN**" appears in gold-orange serif capitals with fiery streaks, about 25% of the frame wide, while the drawn card floats at the right edge at 1.7× size. About a second later the card is in hand and every playable card and able attacker has a cyan outline ([LSV 1630.5-1632.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI&t=1630s)). "**OPPONENT'S TURN**" uses the same size and position in **pale blue-white with blue streaks** ([LSV 1669 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI&t=1669s)). Each banner is visible for about 1-1.5 s. Two cues persist: a **gold sunburst glow behind the active player's portrait**, and a **small row of phase pips** beside the local portrait. The lit pip is **orange on your turn and blue on the opponent's**, with sword, shield and star icons for combat steps ([Numot 1420 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0&t=1420s); [Numot 3405 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0&t=3405s)).

The **pass button** is a pill at the bottom right. It is orange when you act: "Next", with a caption naming the next stop ("To Combat"), or "Resolve" / "Resolve All" when responding to the stack. It is a dark translucent "Opponent's Turn" pill while you wait. Secondary choices ("Cancel", "Mulligan", "Pay 2 Life") are blue. A small ">>" circle under the pill is orange on your turn and blue on theirs ([PowrDragn 850 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=850s); [Numot 3405-3450 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0&t=3405s)). An hourglass with "x0"-"x3" beside each name appears to count remaining timeouts. No running rope or fuse was seen.

**Land plays** show the card enlarged to about 1.6× with a thick orange-gold border near mid-screen. About 2 s later it sits in the back land row ([LSV 900 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI&t=900s); [Paul Cheon 1396-1398 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_ZQRnD2a74&t=1396s)). A shock land opens a dimmed "Pay 2 life?" modal ([PowrDragn 860 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=860s)). **Tapped permanents are not rotated 90°.** They are darkened and desaturated. One pass saw a pale curved-arrow glyph on upright cards ([LSV 1632 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI&t=1632s)); another saw a 10-20° tilt, which may be camera perspective ([Gruul 904.9-908.5 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k&t=904s)).

**Tokens** use an arched, tombstone-shaped frame. Identical tokens merge into one overlapped pile with a single P/T plaque, and they arrive inside a burst of coloured smoke where they land ([PowrDragn 871.25-874 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=871s)). **+1/+1 counters** appear as a dark-blue circle with a white number at each card's top-right, about a sixth of the card's width. When counters are added, white ripple rings spread around every affected card and each P/T plaque flashes white, for about 0.5 s. **Boosted P/T is then shown in blue** ([PowrDragn 1044 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=1044s)).

The **mulligan** screen fans the 7 cards large across mid-screen, under the title "You Go First" or "Opponent Goes First" and a status subtitle. It has a blue "Mulligan" and an orange "Keep" button, with a line of text explaining the bottom-of-library rule ([PowrDragn 1530 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnlFoIcb0Tc&t=1530s); [Paul Cheon 2614-2626 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_ZQRnD2a74&t=2614s)).

**The game ends at the loser's portrait.** After the last hit the board rests about 1.8 s. Then a red glow builds around the losing avatar, a white-hot flash fills about a third of the screen, an orange-red cloud and dark debris burst outward, and an empty pedestal remains as the board dims. From glow to debris takes **about 1.3-1.5 s** ([Gruul 295.3-299.85 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4Nwh4tGq6k&t=295s)). A concession appears to trigger the same explosion ([MTGJace 1050.8-1053.35 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvrV1p1C-B4&t=1050s)). About 2.5-3 s after the killing blow, a gold horizontal lens-flare and a flame-shaped sigil appear, then "**VICTORY**" in gold serif capitals ([Numot 3459-3462 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9OMX_UQqG0&t=3459s)). A loss runs the same sequence in **purple**: a violet flare and sigil, then "**DEFEAT**" in white-silver with a purple glow. The avatar figure and rank emblem follow ([LSV 1669.5-1676 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JpxfGNmClqI&t=1669s)).

## Per-event reproduction plan for the four-seat TTS table

The TTS constraints decide how each Arena effect translates. Particles exist only as pre-authored AssetBundle trigger effects, which take no runtime parameters, so each colour variant needs its own trigger index ([TTSAssetBundleEffects.cs](https://github.com/Berserk-Games/Tabletop-Simulator-Modding/blob/master/Assets/Scripts/TTSAssetBundleEffects.cs)). Global UI offers only Grow/Fade/Slide show-hide tweens with a 0.25 s default ([API: UI attributes](https://api.tabletopsimulator.com/ui/attributes/)). `highlightOn(color, duration)` turns itself off ([API: Object](https://api.tabletopsimulator.com/object/)). There is no camera-shake API. On this table **each `destruct()` costs about 360 ms**, measured locally with PresentMon, so every rig, number and token must be pooled and reused, never spawned and destroyed. Four further points follow from the videos.

First, Arena's effects are sized for a fixed close camera. On a top-down table of 100+ permanents, card-sized effects are small, so seat-level feedback carries most of the weight: the life tracker, a seat ring and per-player screen UI. Second, Arena's flying attack cards should not be copied: `setPositionSmooth` puts a card into physics and sends several networked updates, over a table-length distance. Third, Arena's lines are its most information-dense effect, and they are cheap to reproduce with TTS vector lines (`setVectorLines` on Global or an object: a point list, colour and thickness; [API: Object](https://api.tabletopsimulator.com/object/)). One replicated call can redraw every target, attack and block line. Its cost on this table is unmeasured and needs a PresentMon check. Fourth, because Arena's damage numbers do not scale with the amount, **one number per defender per damage step**, summed, loses nothing and keeps a 15-attacker swing readable. Commander damage gets its own tag.

Priority uses the existing build plan. **P2** is the zero-asset layer (highlights, UI, Object UI, vector lines). **P4** is the pooled VFX-rig bundle. **P5** is polish. High, Med and Low rate importance within each phase.

## What the videos never showed

Several events listed in the brief, or likely to matter in Commander, were not covered. **Mana:** no red or colourless payment, and no floating mana pool. **Zones:** no exile, no aura or equipment attaching, no discard, no mill and no drawn-card trigger beyond the turn-start draw. **Combat keywords:** no trample, first strike, double strike, deathtouch or lifelink, so we don't know whether Arena shows a second damage step or excess carrying through to the player. **Stack:** never deeper than two items, never a spell with several targets, no fizzle from an illegal target, no countered ability, and only one counterspell. That counter's smoke burst might be card-specific rather than the standard "countered" effect. **Spell look:** the meaning of the stack auras (purple swirl, gold ring, blue flame, none) is unknown, and there was no evidence on rarity- or mana-value-scaled effects beyond mana value 3. **Unmeasured detail:** whether the targeting arc animates, and the exact flight easing curves. **Board state:** no large life gain, no counter above 1 and no non-+1/+1 counter, no "×N" number on a token pile, and no confirmation that creature damage stays marked until end of turn. **Turn flow:** no full phase-pip icon set captured at each step, no turn-timer rope, no draw screen and no "opponent conceded" variant. **Brawl and Commander:** nothing at all, so command-zone casts, commander damage and four-player conventions stay unprecedented. **Sound:** none. The tapped look is also unresolved: one pass saw an arrow glyph, the other a slight tilt.

## Conclusion

The research changes the M5 brief. SPEC.md lists "shake, sounds, damage popups, turn banners", but Arena does not shake, and its sound could not be measured. The feel the videos show comes from **lines, colours and well-timed numbers**, nearly all of which TTS can draw with no asset pipeline. So the first M5 milestone should be a zero-asset P2 pass: fix the colour grammar, draw target, attack and block lines, add life-tracker numbers, tween the banners and add the victory/defeat banners. The four particle rigs (death, counter, impact, elimination) wait until the two-client sync test passes. The new requirement that only our table has is **direction across four seats**. Arena's 1v1 camera never had to answer "attacking whom?" or "targeting whom?", so lines are optional polish for Arena and core information for us.

The second takeaway is that Arena's restraint maps well onto TTS's limits. Arena keeps persistent cues tiny and fixed, puts big effects in short bursts, and sums nothing it doesn't need to: damage numbers stay one size and time does the scaling. On a 100-permanent table, the same discipline means summing per defender, coalescing board-wide events, merging identical tokens into one object, and never destroying anything mid-game. Those choices make the table look more like Arena and run faster at the same time.
